# Google EDUtech Asia 2024: Team Recon Drone

We came first place, earning each of us a Chromebook and the Champion accolade.

The competition’s pitch day was held at the Marina Bay Sands Expo and Convention Centre in Singapore. The prompt from EDUtech was to leverage Google’s technology to tackle waste management.

Our team built Recon Drone, a geospatial waste analytics platform. It takes images from various sources like drones or CCTV, and runs them through a supervised fine-tuned YOLOv11 model whose output is used to display the distribution of waste in a geographic location on a React heatmap over time. This heatmap is useful because it allows relevant stakeholders to analyse where waste accumulates in a region over-time, allowing them to root out the cause behind such accumulation or develop targeted strategies to mitigate pollution. For example, a provincial government that knows where trash builds up can concentrate its waste-handling resources in the appropriate location.

Beyond waste management, the same platform can address other environmental issues like illegal logging and wildfire monitoring by simply swapping in a different detection model.

## My Role

I was the lead machine learning engineer and owned the perception stack. The goal was to ship a computer vision model that, using each image from Louis's drone data store, the model detects every piece of trash in it and classifies the type, before finally handing the bounding box detections to Jolie and Jonatas's Flask and Firebase backend for the heatmap.

I first experimented with a ViT classifier in September, and also with training a custom CNN from the ground up. In the end, I went with fine-tuning a YOLOv11 open-source waste datasets I collated from Roboflow. This is because a ViT classifier would’ve been too expensive to serve at scale, and I lacked the technical experience as well as both data and compute resources needed to train a custom classifier CNN from the ground up.

Before any big training runs, I always tested our training code on an NVIDIA L4 from Google Colab, because it's cheaper to fail and make mistakes in Google Colab. Then, once my training code is tested, I fine-tuned a YOLOv11l model on waste classification using NVIDIA H100 spot instances from Google Cloud GCP. I logged our training curves and data to Weights & Biases and uploaded each checkpoint onto Hugging Face.

To fine-tune our computer vision model, I collected 300 thousand images from 25 open-source waste-classification related datasets and merged them on Roboflow. Our final model is a YOLOv11-L, fine-tuned for 9 epochs, reaching 0.381 mAP50 and 0.264 mAP50-95 on our validation set, and forward-passes an image in ~0.015 seconds on an NVIDIA L4.

There were a few hurdles in the process. Although Jolie had experience with CNNs from her coursework whilst I did GPTs for mine ([read more here](/project/gpt-valkyrie)), I just wasn't that experienced then with CNNs and computer vision back then. An example of such a hurdle was that I didn't consolidate the differing class names across the 25 datasets. Because the 31 different class names highly likely made it very hard for the model to learn, as one dataset called glass waste "glass" whilst another called it "glass bottle". This meant the model was penalised inappropriately, which really messed up our initial training runs.

| Class | Source classes folded in |
|---|---|
| Glass | glass, glass bottle |
| Metal | metal, can, aluminium foil, pop tab |
| Plastic | plastic, PET bottle, plastic bag, plastic buoy, plastic container, bottle |
| Styrofoam | styrofoam box, buoy, piece, cup |
| Paper | paper, cardboard, carton |
| Rope | rope, net |
| Food Waste | food, decomposable |
| Other | other litter, straw, fabrics, wood, cigarette, wrapper, lid, bottle cap |
